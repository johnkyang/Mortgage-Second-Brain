# Deal-Advocate Playbook

**Objective (lever):** Revenue + Trust + Leads. Document John's #1 differentiator — getting
the builder to accept the buyer's price, concessions, and buydown — as a **repeatable method**
he can run on this builder **and future ones** (which is how he beats the sell-out clock).

## Why it works (the two-part moat)
1. **The builder trusts John's suggestions** — his read carries weight.
2. **John holds the qualification math** — as the lender, he knows exactly what terms the
   buyer needs to qualify. He turns that into a credible case the builder believes.

An outside lender has neither the trust nor the standing to make this case. This is
structurally John's to win.

## The core move
**Translate "what the buyer needs to qualify and buy" → "the term the builder must give to
make this sale close."** You're not asking for a favor; you're showing the builder the path
to a closed home (and away from fallout). Frame as **builder's ally**, not buyer's lobbyist.

## The 5-step method
1. **Diagnose the qualification gap.** Run the buyer's numbers: what payment / price /
   credit do they need to qualify? Identify the exact lever — price reduction (DTI/LTV),
   buydown (payment), or concession (cash-to-close).
2. **Pick the cheapest "yes" for the builder.** Often a **rate buydown** or closing-cost
   concession costs the builder less than a price cut but solves the buyer's payment — easier
   to get approved. Lead with the ask that's smallest for the builder, biggest for the buyer.
3. **Build the case (data + closing logic).** "Buyer qualifies at $X payment; a 2-1 buydown
   gets them there. Fund it and this closes on time. Without it, they walk and you re-market
   the home." Make it about *certainty of the sale*, not generosity.
4. **Leverage the trust + protect it.** Because the builder trusts your read, only bring
   cases you believe — never cry wolf. Your credibility is the asset; protecting it keeps
   every future ask powerful.
5. **Close the loop + capture proof.** Get the term, fund the loan, and **log what you won**
   (concession $, buydown structure) → fuels content + sales proof + the next case.

## Ammunition = your program toolkit
The more ways you can get a buyer to qualify (DPA, buydowns, non-QM, bank-statement), the
more cases you can build. Program breadth = more "here's how this buyer qualifies" wins.
→ [[DPA_STRATEGY.md]] and John's non-QM/DSCR/bank-statement toolkit are direct inputs.

## ★ Replicate it (beat the finite clock)
This method is **not specific to one builder.** The same motion — earn trust → hold the
qualification math → build the case → protect credibility — can be deployed with **new
builders**. Turning a one-off relationship into a repeatable builder-acquisition motion is
the durable answer to the ~12–18 month sell-out risk.
- Extension play: because this builder trusts John, pursue **preferred-lender status on the
  builder's NEXT projects** before these sell out.
- New-builder play: lead the pitch with this method (see [[BUILDER_STRATEGY.md]]).

## Make it content (it's authority gold)
- "How I got my buyer $X in concessions + a buydown the bank couldn't."
- "Why your new-construction lender should be negotiating your deal."
→ feeds the #0 content pillar in [[CONTENT_STRATEGY.md]].

## Compliance
- Cases to the builder must be **honest** (real qualification facts) — credibility is the
  whole moat. Never misrepresent a buyer's qualification.
- No guarantees of specific concessions/rates in marketing; mark specifics `TODO: verify`.
- Keep any builder/lender arrangements RESPA-compliant.

## Open questions for John (to deepen + replicate)
1. How did you earn the builder's trust originally? (so we can replicate it with new builders)
2. Which ask lands most often — price, concession, or buydown?
3. Do you have 1–2 specific recent wins we can turn into proof/content?
