# Cost of Waiting (Rate Objection Reframe)

**One-line definition:** A reframe for the "rates are too high, I'll wait" objection that
shifts the buyer from *price of the rate* to *total cost of inaction*.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the most common first-time-buyer stalls. A clean reframe
turns hesitation into a pre-approval conversation.

## The idea
Waiting has costs buyers don't see: continued rent (building zero equity), home-price
appreciation, lost tax/wealth-building time, and the crowd that floods back in when rates
drop (more competition, higher prices, bidding wars). "Marry the house, date the rate" —
you can refinance a rate, you can't retro-buy at today's price.

## How to use it
- **When:** buyer says "I'll wait for rates to drop."
- **How:** ask questions first (consultative) — "What would need to be true for you to feel
  good buying?" — then reframe to total cost of waiting + the refi option.
- **Example language:** "Totally fair. Can I show you what waiting actually costs vs. what
  buying now + refinancing later looks like? That way you're deciding with the full picture."
