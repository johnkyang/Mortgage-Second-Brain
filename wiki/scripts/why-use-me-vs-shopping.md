# Why Use Me vs. Shopping the Loan

**Goal:** Keep a buyer you pre-approved from taking the pre-approval and shopping the loan
elsewhere. Critical for the builder account — **no incentive lock-in**, buyer's choice, so
retention is won on relationship + trust + certainty.

**When to use:** Proactively right after pre-approval, and reactively when a buyer says
"I'm going to shop around" or "I found a lower rate online."

## Principle
Don't get defensive about rate. **Reframe from "lowest rate" to "best deal + certain,
on-time close"** — and lead with the thing no outside lender can match: John helps get the
buyer the builder's price, concessions, and buy-down credits
(see [[wiki/concepts/builder-deal-advocate]]). Set the expectation *before* a competitor's
quote shows up, so you're the trusted advisor, not the one playing catch-up.

## The strongest hook (deal-advocate)
> "Quick thing most buyers don't realize: I'm not just your lender — I work with the sales
> team to get the builder to come down on price, add concessions, and fund a rate buydown.
> An online lender can't do any of that. If you take a slightly lower quote elsewhere, you
> can actually lose far more in concessions and buydown than you'd ever save on rate."

## Proactive framing (say this at pre-approval — get ahead of the shopping)
> "[Name], one heads-up so you're never caught off guard: once you're pre-approved, you may
> get calls or see online rates that look lower. That's normal — but a quoted rate isn't a
> locked rate, and the cheapest quote often comes with hidden costs, slower service, or a
> deal that falls apart near closing. On a new-construction timeline, a blown close date is
> the real risk. My job is to get you to the keys on time, with no surprises. If you ever see
> a number that looks better, **send it to me before you act** — I'll tell you honestly if
> it's real and apples-to-apples. Deal?"

This earns the right to see competitor quotes — most buyers will, if you framed it first.

## Reactive: "I found a lower rate online / with another lender"
1. **Validate, don't defend:** "Smart to compare — you should. Send me the full quote (the
   Loan Estimate, not just the rate) and I'll break it down with you."
2. **Compare apples to apples:** rate vs. points/credits, lender fees, lock period, and
   *whether they can actually close on your timeline.* A low rate with 2 points isn't lower.
3. **Reframe to certainty:** "On a builder close date, the worst outcome isn't a slightly
   higher rate — it's a lender who can't perform and costs you the home or your deposit."
4. **Offer to compete honestly:** "If it's a genuine apples-to-apples better deal, I'll tell
   you, and I'll do my best to match or beat it. I'd rather earn it than lose you to a quote
   that won't hold."

## Compliance guardrails
- Never quote/guarantee a specific rate, APR, or "savings" you can't back. Mark specifics
  `TODO: verify` and only quote within current lock/pricing.
- No guarantees of approval or "lowest rate" claims you can't substantiate.
- Compare using the official Loan Estimate; keep all statements truthful.

## Why it works
- Concept: reframes a price objection into a risk/certainty decision (see
  [[wiki/concepts/cost-of-waiting]] for the sibling reframe).
- Emotional trigger: fear of losing the home / a deal falling apart > desire to save a few bps.

## Variations
- Text version (shorter), in-person version (more consultative). Build as tested.

## Performance notes
- Track: how often buyers send competitor quotes back (sign the framing worked) + win rate
  after a competing quote.
